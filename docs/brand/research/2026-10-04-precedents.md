# Precedents: how category-defining brands earned their weight

Date: 2026-10-04. Part of the brand v2 research (`docs/brand/BRAND.md`).

**How this was sourced.** A research agent searched the web. Several primary sites blocked direct fetches, so many claims come from search-result extracts. **[unverified]** marks a claim that rests only on secondary sources.

## What the winners did

- **Apple, "1984" and "Think Different".** "1984" named an enemy (conformity) and sold liberation, without specs. The board wanted it pulled; it aired anyway [1]. "Think Different" restated values before the iMac proved them [2][3].
- **Nike.** "If you have a body, you are an athlete." The user is the athlete; Nike enables. "Find Your Greatness" made a 12-year-old jogger the hero [4][5][6].
- **Airbnb.** The founders spent a year boiling the company down to "belonging". The Bélo was made to be drawable and customisable. It was mocked on launch day and survived because the mission held [7][8][9].
- **Salesforce, "No Software".** Defined the category by its enemy, with a staged protest at a rival's conference. The product made the slogan literally true [10][11].
- **Tesla's "Secret Master Plan".** A staged, checkable roadmap was the marketing [12]. The caution: a brand fused with its founder takes the founder's damage [15].
- **Stripe.** "Increase the GDP of the internet." It treats docs and craft as marketing, and Stripe Press books as brand artifacts. Visible craft signals reliability [16][17][18].
- **Notion and Figma.** Grew through community: ambassadors, templates and community members on stage [20][21][22][23].
- **Palantir.** Named its core abstraction, the Ontology, and sold through proof (hands-on bootcamps) rather than pitch [24][25].
- **Shopify.** "Let's Make You a Business": the brand is a verb done for the user [26][27].
- **Patagonia.** Credibility from costly structural acts: "Don't Buy This Jacket", and "Earth is now our only shareholder" [28][29].
- **Disney.** Worlds that compound around one core. Walt said of Disneyland that it "will never be completed as long as there's imagination left" [30][31].
- **Minecraft and Roblox.** The brand is what users make; Roblox creators earned more than $1B in a year [32][33].

## What the losers did

- **Humane AI Pin.** Errors in its own launch video, reviews calling it "thoroughly unfinished", and returns outpacing sales. It sold for $116M against more than $230M raised. With an AI product, the first public answer becomes the brand [34][35][36][37].
- **Juicero.** One simple test (squeezing the pack by hand) exposed staged craft [39].
- **Quibi.** $1.75B raised; closed in six months [38].
- **Google Glass.** The consent of bystanders shaped the brand ("Glasshole") [40].
- **Meta.** Renamed itself after a future that had not arrived; Reality Labs has lost more than $70B [41].

## Ten principles

1. Name what you free people from, not the feature.
2. Make the user the hero; write the promise as a verb done for them.
3. Prove before you proclaim.
4. Stage the ambition publicly, as a ladder whose rungs can be checked.
5. Name the core abstraction. For solve: **the record**.
6. Make costly, structural commitments that can't be faked.
7. Let users build the world, and make their outcomes the marketing.
8. One master brand with descriptive names under it; never rename for a speculative future [50][51].
9. Marks earn meaning over time: design for drawability and endurance. Mastercard dropped its name only after more than 80% recognised the symbol [43].
10. Make the brand stand on its own beyond the founder.

## Identity lessons

- **Mark:** drawable, and works at favicon size (Google's G) [46].
- **Wordmark:** lowercase, eventually in a bespoke typeface (Uber Move, Product Sans) [46][47].
- **Proof device:** a record seal carrying an ID and timestamp, shown only on verified outcomes. Pair it with one reserved meaning colour, used sparingly (Uber's Safety Blue) [47]. Simulated evidence gets a visibly different treatment.
- **Motion:** consistent paths and easing [46].
- **Sound:** a short confirmation sound, like Mastercard's acceptance sound, Netflix's "ta-dum" or Intel's bong [42][44][45].
