# SOLVE brand direction: the construct

Date: 2026-10-04. Status: **proposed**, from the founder's conversation with Claude. The founder decides. The Build Book's brand lock (§2, "Tony Stark HUD") stays in force until the founder changes it. This doc says what to change it to and why.

## The idea

**Stay in your seat. The world builds around you, and every result is real.**

The founder's image: the Matrix construct scene, where the chair stays and the world changes around it. Here, a person sits in an empty apartment at a computer. As they work, the room becomes a pen factory with a clean white floor, the line running, and them still coding in the same chair while it produces. Then a car studio, a kitchen at rush hour, a stage. One world for every product angle.

This is the vision, filmed:
- *"The player is the center. Sovereign is the world that grows around them."*
- *"They don't open Sovereign and browse 500 features. They ask for a way."*
- *"AI can flow everywhere the player authorizes. Authority cannot."*
- *"…execute in the real world, record what actually happened."*

The Build Book's launch copy already uses the red pill: *"every claim logged before the outcome."*

## Why it fits people right now

The figures are from 2025–2026 surveys; the sources are listed at the end.

- **Using AI more, trusting it less.** Half of Americans are more concerned than excited about AI, and only 10% more excited. About half think it will worsen creative thinking and relationships. Trust in AI search fell from 82% to 54% in a year. The top worries are wrong answers driving decisions (71%), losing their own skills (63%) and losing human connection (61%).
- **The backlash against "AI slop".** 32% stopped buying from a brand they suspected of AI content in 2026, up from 11%. 78% say AI makes ads feel less authentic.
- **No trust for agents with money.** 55% are not comfortable letting AI buy for them.
- **Life.** Money is the top stress. 62% of Gen Z and 52% of millennials plan side income; burnout is common. 72% say there are too many subscriptions, and 78% want one app.

| What people feel | What SOLVE says |
|---|---|
| "I don't want more AI, I want my life to work." | Lead with the person and a real thing getting made, not with AI. |
| "AI will make me worse at things." | You stay the author: you're in the chair, deciding. |
| "AI content is fake." | Proof. Every story ends on a real record. |
| "I won't let AI touch my money." | Authority stays with you. Nothing spends, signs or publishes without your yes, and you can erase anything. |
| "I need money and I'm exhausted." | From an empty room to a running operation, with no income promises (Build Book §10). |
| "Too many apps." | One world, not 500 features. |

## What history says

Every big technology passed through hype, then fear, then trust, then the mainstream. The winners:
1. **Sold the outcome, not the technology.** Edison sold light. Kodak: *"You press the button, we do the rest."*
2. **Built the trust system.** Underwriters Laboratories, eBay feedback, PayPal buyer protection. SOLVE's record is that trust system for AI acting in the real world.
3. **Made the new feel familiar.** The "horseless carriage"; the desktop and trash can.
4. **Sided with the individual.** Apple's 1984 ad: *"the computer for the rest of us."* Sovereign is the individual's world.
5. **Made starting cheap.** Singer's installments, Ford's price. SOLVE's version is bring your own AI, so there's no new AI bill.

**What lost:**
- **Hype without proof:** the dot-com bubble, crypto.
- **Harvesting attention:** social media.
- **Technology that helps only the owner:** what the Luddites fought.

So never position SOLVE as replacing people. *You own the factory now.* The best positioning moment is the trust trough, which is where AI is now.

## The ontology underneath, felt and never named

The world in SOLVE doesn't appear by magic. It **assembles**: people, machines, suppliers and steps snap into place and connect, because SOLVE keeps a living model of the person's world (what they have, who they know, what things take). In the ad, that's how the factory arrives, piece by piece. On screen, it's the World. In copy, it's a hint: "it understands how your world fits together." The word "ontology" stays internal.

## Rules

**Do:**
- **The person and their seat** are the one constant in every frame.
- **Lock the framing.** The camera never moves; the world changes.
- **Show assembly, not glitch.** Things build into place, physically.
- **End on proof:** a shipped order, a printed part, a confirmed supplier, a record.
- **Real footage of the person.** Generated imagery only where the world changes, kept grounded and realistic, and disclosed.
- **Write in terse, confident plain words** (Build Book §2).
- **Keep screens calm:** the World and the person first, few panels, no chrome for its own sake.

**Don't:**
- Glitch effects, particle swarms, HUD rings or dashboards on marketing surfaces.
- The words "agents", "superintelligence", "revolutionary".
- Income promises or projections without evidence (Build Book §10).
- Leading with the SOV token. *Reasons to use before reasons to trade.*
- Simulated activity presented as real. Arcadia stays labeled as a sandbox.
- The red chair or any other copied Matrix imagery. The seat is the person's own desk.

## The ad: shot list

Every shot is locked off at the same framing: the person at the desk, centered.

1. **Empty apartment, night.** Desk, laptop, a lamp. They type into SOLVE: "I want to make pens." Silence.
2. **The construct.** On a keystroke, the walls fall away to a clean white floor, and a pen factory builds itself around the desk: molding machines, the line starting, workers, boxes with the brand. They keep working.
3. **Proof beat.** A close-up of the screen: "Order 1042 shipped." A real record.
4. **Montage of keystrokes.** Each one a new world at the same framing: a car design studio (clay model, designers), a restaurant kitchen at rush, a streaming stage, an agency floor, a house going up.
5. **Return.** The empty apartment again. On the screen is their World, holding all of it.
6. **Super:** *SOLVE. The world builds around you.*

**The 15-second social cut (the 3D-print mini).** "Asked SOLVE for a phone stand." The design turns on screen, the printer runs, the part is in hand, and the record reads "Designed by Nova, printed Oct 4."

## Production pipeline

- **The plate.** One locked-off shot of the founder at the desk in the empty apartment: phone on a tripod, 10-second takes, natural light plus the laptop glow.
- **The character.** 15–20 photos of the founder, used for a consistent likeness in Higgsfield.
- **The worlds.** Higgsfield stills at the exact plate framing, then 3–5 second image-to-video clips. If a real factory is reachable, film the hero world for real.
- **Transitions.** A whiteout or assembly cut in the edit. On the web, a unicorn.studio transition.
- **Website.** Lenis smooth scroll pins the seat while scrolling swaps the world behind it, ending on "Enter your World".

### Higgsfield prompt pattern, one per world

> Locked-off medium-wide shot, same framing as the reference plate. The person from the reference sits at a plain desk with a laptop at the center of the frame, working. Around them, [WORLD], with a clean white concrete floor. Realistic, natural light, documentary feel, no text, no glow, no sci-fi effects.

Values for `[WORLD]`:
- A pen manufacturing floor: injection-molding machines, a conveyor line running, workers in grey uniforms, stacked branded boxes.
- A car design studio: a full-size clay model, designers sculpting, large monitors with sketches.
- A restaurant kitchen at dinner rush: chefs plating, steam, tickets printing.
- A streaming stage: lights, cameras, a small crew, a live monitor.
- An agency floor: desks, people on calls, a wall screen with a campaign.
- A house under construction: a timber frame, workers, an open sky.

## What changes in the product

- **The bot screen is calm.** Character on a lit floor, plain words. The HUD effects and particle cloud were removed on 2026-10-04.
- **The World is the stage where things assemble**, and the first real loop is "make a real thing": ask, design, make, prove.
- **Marketing and onboarding open on the person and the seat**, never on AI.

## Decisions for the founder

1. Replace the Build Book's "Tony Stark HUD" brand lock with this direction, or adjust it.
2. Choose which door opens first: the Build Book's Whop verification wedge, or "make real things". Both run on the same proof engine.
3. Film the plate.
