# Scale AI, and what it means for solve

The founder (2026-10-05): *"look at scale ai"*. This is research for the business plan (`docs/plans/2026-10-05-business-leverage-outreach.md` §5).

- Figures are as the linked sources report them; solve hasn't verified them.
- Per-person numbers marked *(arithmetic)* are worked out here from those figures.
- Nothing here changes canon or sets a fee rate; fee rates are parked.

## What Scale is

Scale sells human work to AI labs, enterprises and government: labelling, ranking and writing model answers (RLHF), red-teaming and evaluation. It charges per task or by contract, and pays a crowd of contributors (Remotasks, then Outlier) less than it charges. The markup gives an estimated gross margin above 50% ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-does-scale-ai-make-money-data-labeling-rlhf-and-the-meta-14-3b-stake)).

- **Size:** about $870M of revenue in 2024 ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/how-does-scale-ai-make-money-data-labeling-rlhf-and-the-meta-14-3b-stake)), with an estimated run-rate near $1.5B at the end of that year ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/scale-ai/)).
- **The crowd:** Outlier says it has paid contributors over $600M in total ([Outlier](https://outlier.ai/new-home)).
- **The Meta deal (June 2025):**
  - Meta paid about $14B for 49% and hired the founder, Alexandr Wang. Jason Droege now runs Scale.
  - Meta signed a five-year contract worth at least $450M a year, and Scale lost OpenAI.
  - Rivals say Scale is now passed over in labelling bids because of Meta's stake.
  - Droege projects 2026 revenue above $1B. He is turning Scale toward building AI applications for enterprises and government: about $200M a year already, which he expects to outgrow labelling within 18 months. He says the reason is that the labelling market is slowing ([KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/scale-ai-revenue-expected-to-exceed-1-billion-in-2026-as-enterprise-ai-applications-grow)).

## The trouble with the crowd

- **Lawsuits:** contributors sued in December 2024 and twice in January 2025, saying they were misclassified as contractors.
  - One Outlier worker said she worked about 10 hours a day but was paid for five, because time spent reading instructions and training wasn't paid.
  - That worked out to about $15 an hour, under California's minimum wage ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/09/scale-ai-hit-by-its-second-employee-wage-lawsuit-in-less-than-a-month), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/22/scale-ai-is-facing-a-third-worker-lawsuit-in-about-a-month)).
- **The settlement:** $12.5M from Scale AI, Smart Ecosystem and HireArt, for contributors living in California from 2020-12-10 to 2026-02-28 (*McKinney v. Scale AI*). The claims covered minimum wage, overtime, expenses, breaks and wage statements ([Open Class Actions](https://openclassactions.com/settlements/employment-law/scale-ai-contributor-misclassification-class-action-settlement.php), [ClaimDepot](https://www.claimdepot.com/settlements/mckinney-scaleai-settlement)).

## Who is winning the same market

- **Mercor:** a marketplace of paid experts (physics, finance, law, medicine) who make training data for AI labs and large companies.
  - Raised $350M at a $10B valuation in October 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/27/mercor-quintuples-valuation-to-10b-with-350M-series-c/)).
  - Passed $2B of gross annualized revenue in June 2026 and pays 60–70% of it to contractors, so net revenue is roughly $600–800M.
  - Pays over $2M a day to more than 30,000 weekly active contractors ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/137121-mercor-doubles-to-2b-gross-revenue-run-rate-as-ai-labs-buy-expert-data/)).
  - *(Arithmetic)* That is about **$20,000–27,000 of net revenue a year for each weekly active contractor**, and about $24,000 a year paid to each.
- **Surge AI:** $1.2B of revenue in 2024, profitable and bootstrapped since 2021, with about 50,000 expert contractors and 130 staff. In 2025 it sought a valuation of $15–25B ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/surge-ai)).
  - *(Arithmetic)* That is about $24,000 of revenue for each contractor.

## What it means for solve

1. **The network's $10,000 a person is already beaten in this market.** Mercor makes roughly twice that, net, for each weekly active contractor.
   - That counts active people, not everyone who signs up, so it supports the plan's split: operators carry the number, and it is measured both per active person and across everyone.
   - The value flowing through is in the same range as §5's table: about $24,000 a year paid to each Mercor contractor, and $100,000 for an operator billing 1,000 hours at $100.
2. **The winners compete on trusted work, not volume.** Mercor and Surge grew on vetted experts and quality while Scale's crowd was in court, and Scale lost a lab over who owns it. solve's whole design is trust in the work:
   - who did it, on the record;
   - evidence with its basis (self-reported, witnessed, artifact, independently verified);
   - Earn Vault releases only for independently verified outcomes (constitution Part V).
3. **Do the opposite of what got Scale sued:**
   - pay agreed before the work and shown on the record;
   - no unpaid instructions or training;
   - no rate changed after the fact;
   - the contributor sees the same record the buyer sees.

   solve's ledger makes that checkable rather than promised. Whether contributors are contractors or something else is a legal question for stage 3, before any money moves.
4. **Neutrality is the asset.** Scale lost OpenAI when Meta took 49%. solve already works with whichever assistant a person brings (Claude, ChatGPT, Codex). Ownership, data and the connector should stay neutral across labs, and that belongs in any future funding terms.
5. **Where Scale is heading is where solve already is.** Scale is moving from labelling toward AI applications that do work for organizations. That is the World loop with routes, outreach and proof, but run for the person and on their record.
6. **Kids and paid work:** paid tasks for minors bring child labour and COPPA rules. The `young` profile stays out of paid work until there is legal advice. Paid work is for the standard and operator profiles.
7. **Crypto fits the payout problem.** These crowds are global, and paying people across many countries is slow and costly. Stablecoin payouts to a wallet the person proved they control (stage 2, built) are the natural rail, but only at stage 3, behind the founder's approval and legal review.

## The smallest build this suggests (not started)

**Paid work in a World.**

- **What it is:** a need posted in a World carries an agreed price. It goes from claimed, to done, to verified by someone other than the doer, and the payment owed shows on the record.
- **What it reuses:** kernel tasks, evidence and Codex's resource map. There is no new concept.
- **Money:** Practice money only, labelled as such, until stage 3.
- **The founder's own use:** posting paid work in the "Build solve" World for the people helping build it.

It waits on the founder's word, alongside the queued slices (GitHub into "Build solve", Packs, Export).
