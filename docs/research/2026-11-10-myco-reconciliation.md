# Myco reconciled against SOLVE

*Myco — The Mycelium: Connect, Learn, Grow Together*, by Rex Jannus (the founder's brother, who also
built MCPvault). Received 2026-11-10. Confirmed by the founder: "that's my brother… it's like we've
been building separate things to the same app."

## The finding

Two brothers built toward one conviction from opposite ends. SOLVE started from the person — make my
reality provable and mine. Myco started from the community — people within walking distance who can
count on each other. These are one need at two scales. SOLVE's kernel is the person half, built.
Myco is the community half, imagined. Neither works alone.

## Where they are already the same

| Myco | SOLVE |
|---|---|
| No baked-in skill tree, governance or currency; communities build them | The product supplies no vocabulary of its own; a community's skill tree is a set of **type definitions** (`define_type`, applied to staging) |
| The spore: one person, portable, recoverable but not resettable | SOV ID on an append-only, hash-linked history; the scoped-recovery decision of 2026-11-09 matches almost word for word |
| Grove → forest → mycelium, federation opt-in at every level | Scopes, shared Spaces, grants that are explicit and revocable |
| SDK, plugins, "communities ship the features" | SOLVE Connect (`docs/plans/2026-11-10-solve-connect.md`) |
| "Check-ins verified by peers", photo proof | Counterparty confirmation (verified on staging) and evidence |

## The bridge Myco needs most

Myco's XP economy rests on peer-verified check-ins. As written that is the weak point: a check-in is a
claim, and XP for claims is gameable. SOLVE's confirmation makes it credible — another person confirms
the act, as evidence authored by them, and it can be disputed. **XP awarded only for confirmed acts**
is a credential a stranger can trust; that is what makes Myco's badges worth anything outside the app.

## Conflicts to resolve, not ignore

- **Public XP, leaderboards, titles, and votes weighted by contribution** conflict with recorded
  canon: no public popularity scores; credibility contextual, source-backed and contestable. Weighting
  governance by XP converts time-on-platform into political power.
- **Paid XP boosts** let people buy what credentials are meant to prove.
- **Household locations, live status, "in need of help" and proximity alerts, in a product launching
  with homeschool families.** Children's locations on a shared map is the most serious risk in the
  document. Canon already keeps public discovery and contact away from minors until safeguards exist.
- **Defense branches** (marksmanship, tactical, surveillance, escape and evasion) **combined with "we
  cannot honor lawful intercept"**: legal civic traditions, but exactly the combination app-store
  review, payment processors and trust-and-safety act on. A platform-survival risk to plan for before
  launch.
- **Custodial USDC wallet** meets the same money-transmission licensing as the SOV card.

## What Myco brings that SOLVE lacks

- **The real-world map** as a primary surface. The kernel already stores location *with its source*
  (`location_truth`: declared, device-observed, provider-derived…); nothing shows it yet.
- **Encrypted messaging via Matrix** — the textbook Connect integration: integrate, don't rebuild.
- **A game layer people want to open.** SOLVE's experience is trustworthy; Myco's is fun.

## The reconciliation

One foundation, two builders. Myco becomes the community-and-geography experience built on SOLVE:
spores are SOV IDs, groves are shared Spaces, skill trees are types, verified XP is confirmation,
the SDK is Connect, Matrix plugs in through Connect, and the map is a new surface over records that
already carry location. Built separately, Myco would need its own identity, authority, provenance and
export — the parts that took SOLVE longest to get right.
